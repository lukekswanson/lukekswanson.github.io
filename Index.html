The Challenge

Getting paid shouldn't feel like filing taxes

Small business owners need to get paid. It's the whole point. But QuickBooks Payments buried that simple job under jargon, unclear choices, and flows that felt designed for accountants — not the actual people using them.

My job: lead content design across the entire payments experience. From the moment someone signs up through their ongoing use of invoicing, AI features, and a new Mailchimp integration.

Discovery

We watched people get confused. Then we fixed it.

I ran usability sessions with small business owners at every stage of the payments journey. I paid attention to where they paused, re-read, and gave up. Then I did a full content audit — 60+ screens — to map every place the language was inconsistent or unclear.

The pattern was consistent: merchants didn't know which payment method to pick first, the verification flow felt like a legal minefield, and the invoice panel was organized for internal logic instead of how owners actually think. Six of 7 usability participants said Payments was the most important feature — but most couldn't complete setup without help.

"I don't know which one to pick. What if I pick the wrong one and can't change it?" — Merchant, usability session on payment method selection

Strategy

A system, not just a rewrite

Instead of fixing screens one at a time, I built a content framework around where merchants were in their journey. New signup? Here's what you need to know right now — nothing else. First transaction sent? Let's acknowledge that, then tell you what's next. Each stage got targeted messaging instead of a generic blast.

For the invoice panel, I drove a structural rethink: out with the sprawling settings panel, in with a hierarchy that puts the most-used controls first. For the AI assistant, I defined when it should speak, what it should say, and — just as important — when to stay quiet.

Execution

In the weeds, on purpose

I worked in Figma alongside designers across several simultaneous workstreams: Payments onboarding, the invoice panel redesign, AI-powered suggestions, late fees, recurring payments, and a Mailchimp payment link integration.

The Challenge

Nobody had written the rules yet

Intuit was moving fast into AI — building suggestions, agents, and LLM-generated content across QuickBooks. The problem? No one had figured out how any of it should sound. Writers were making inconsistent calls. Engineers were writing prompt templates without content review. And "good AI content" meant something different to everyone.

I built the guidance, training, and governance infrastructure from scratch. No template for this one.

Discovery

The problem wasn't tone. It was cognitive load.

I audited AI-generated content across QuickBooks: invoice reminders, agentic suggestions, chat responses, proactive tips. The failures were consistent — AI was saying too much, too soon, to people who just wanted to get something done.

I also reviewed the prompt engineering infrastructure — including the Python codebase behind invoice reminder generation — to understand where content decisions were being made in code instead of by writers.

The biggest insight: agents shouldn't show off. They should do the work and get out of the way.

Strategy

Principles, patterns, training — in that order

I structured the work in three layers. First, core principles: how agents should speak (first person where it helps, passive voice where the agent's presence would feel intrusive), what to prioritize (customer benefit, not AI capability), and what to never do (unsolicited education, exhaustive lists, detailed previews of future AI actions).

Second, reusable content patterns for specific AI moments — welcome messages, proactive suggestions, error recovery, confirmations, disclosures. Each one came with before-and-after examples from real product copy.

Third, training materials the whole org could actually use. Visual, concrete, short enough to read in a sitting. Because guidance nobody reads doesn't help anyone.

Execution

From Google Doc to living standard

The core document — "Content for GBSG Intuit Assist and agentic experiences" — became the canonical reference for AI content across the Payments and Invoicing team. Voice principles, cognitive load rules, disclosure patterns, and a clear line between "speak" and "stay quiet."

The training slides were designed to be immediately useful: sentence case rules, "worth the space" frameworks for what AI should say, and before/after examples grounded in content people had actually shipped. No hypotheticals.
